## SECTION 4 — WALTER HINES PAGE

[LEFT: Oil portrait, László, London 1917 — slides in on scroll]
[RIGHT: Text block]

EYEBROW: THE MAN WHO BUILT IT

HEADLINE:
Ambassador. Editor. Visionary.
*He named it Garran Hill.*

BODY:
Walter Hines Page was born in Cary, North Carolina, in 1855. He co-founded Doubleday, Page & Co. He edited The Atlantic Monthly. He nominated Woodrow Wilson for the presidency at the 1912 convention.

Wilson appointed him Ambassador to the Court of St. James. He served throughout the First World War — three thousand miles from the house being built for him in his native Moore County.

From London, he wrote home constantly. Not about diplomacy.

PULL QUOTE (gold, Cormorant italic, large):
*"The farm — the farm — the farm."*

BODY CONTINUED:
He purchased the original tract — nearly a thousand acres of timbered land — in February 1913. He named it Garran Hill. Construction was supervised by his son Ralph, following plans from a Boston architectural firm. The craftsmen came from Leonard Tufts' crew — the same men building Pinehurst two miles away.

He returned to North Carolina in December 1918. He died ten days later.

CLOSING LINE (small caps, cream, letter-spaced):
HE NEVER SPENT A NIGHT HERE.

HEADLINE:
Thomas O'Shea of Durham.

BODY:
Architect Thomas O'Shea led a complete interior restoration. General contractor: Dennis Dunagan. The interior was gutted. All plumbing and electrical replaced. Custom Marvin windows made to match the 1916 originals. A five-zone climate system engineered specifically to protect the original architecture.

All seven carved mantels saved. Most of the original oak flooring lifted, repaired, and relaid. The period-accurate portico brick took three months to source.

Fifteen architectural drawings survive. They transfer with the property.
